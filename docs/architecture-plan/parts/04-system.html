14 · UX principles & statesSystem
14UX principles & states
Quality lives in the states, not just the finished screens
Clarity before decoration

One primary job and one clear next action per screen.

Task-first dashboards

Actionable items before vanity metrics.

Progressive disclosure

Farming, procurement and finance detail expands on demand.

Mobile-first for producers

Large targets, short forms, no wide tables.

Offline is normal

Save locally, show sync state, never block on signal.

Trust through status

One status language everywhere.

Real content in design

Ghanaian crops, farms, GH₵ prices, real dates.

Accessible by default

AA contrast, labels, focus, non-colour status cues.

15UX quality check
What we would change, stated openly

An independent pass over the planned UX. Each recommendation is explicit and needs approval; nothing here has been slipped in silently.

QuestionVerdictRecommendation
Is navigation too large?In placesAdmin: group 16 items into 6 sections. B2B: 9 → 7. Farmer: 10 → 4 tabs + Log harvest.
Is the farmer portal simple enough?Not in v1Bottom-bar app with one primary action, offline saves, and local-language labels (Twi, Ewe, Ga) as a C option.
Does B2B have the right emphasis?Yes, with fixesKeep it operational. Rename RFQs to Bids (PRS) and merge Procurement into Source.
Is the consumer portal too dashboard-heavy?AvoidedNo metrics. Orders first, Buy again, no subscriptions in P1.
Is the admin logically structured?FlatGroup by Marketplace / Network / Trust / Money / System; add a Verification queue (FR-1.1).
Is VSLA Phase 1 scoped correctly?YesAdd Group rules (FR-2.1). Keep credit, insurance and investment out until P3.
Is traceability at the right level for P1?Over-scopedCapture IDs and show provenance in P1; public QR page and logistics events in P2.
